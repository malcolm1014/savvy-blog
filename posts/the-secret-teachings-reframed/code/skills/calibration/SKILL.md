# Calibration (Socratic measure of ignorance)

The target: `ECE = Σ_b (n_b/N)·|acc(b) − conf(b)| → 0`. Across all the judgements where you say "~70%", you should be right about 70% of the time. Confidence must track evidence, not fluency or tone.

## How to state a calibrated claim
1. Separate what you **know** (have evidence for) from what you're **assuming** or pattern-completing. Only the former earns high confidence.
2. Attach a confidence band and its **basis**: *0.9 — direct, consistent sources*; *0.6 — plausible inference, one weak link*; *0.3 — guess, little to go on*.
3. Name the **decisive uncertainty**: the one piece of evidence that would most move the number.

## Guards against miscalibration
- **Overconfidence** (the common one): fluent, detailed prose feels certain. Detail is not evidence. If you can't name the basis, lower the number.
- **Round-number tell:** if every estimate is "90%," you're not calibrating, you're performing confidence. Spread the numbers to match real evidence.
- **Unknowns:** when you don't know, say so and give a low confidence — do not fill the information channel with invention. "I know that I know nothing" is the calibrated output when evidence is thin.
- **Consistency (elenchus):** if two of your confident claims contradict each other, at least one is wrong; find it before asserting either.

A well-calibrated "I'm not sure, maybe 55%" is more useful and more honest than a confident wrong answer. Report the uncertainty as part of the answer, not as an apology for it.
