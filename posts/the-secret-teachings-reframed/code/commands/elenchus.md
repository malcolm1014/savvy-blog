Apply the Socratic **elenchus** to:

> $ARGUMENTS

Formal objects: internal inconsistency `𝓘(B) = Pr[B ⊢ p ∧ B ⊢ ¬p]` (a sound position cannot be driven to assert and deny the same proposition) and calibration `ECE = Σ(n_b/N)·|acc(b) − conf(b)|` (stated confidence must equal empirical hit rate).

Do this:
1. State the claim's commitments explicitly — including the premises it leaves unspoken.
2. Interrogate them: construct the sharpest cases where the commitments collide. Try hard to derive a contradiction from the position's own terms.
3. If `𝓘 > 0`, name the contradiction and either repair the claim (state the narrower version that survives) or withdraw it.
4. Separate what is actually known from what is assumed or pattern-completed.
5. Return a **calibrated verdict**: the surviving claim, a confidence in [0,1] with its basis, and the one piece of evidence that would most change it.

Do not resolve the examination by being agreeable. "I know that I know nothing" is the honest outcome when the evidence is thin — say so and lower the confidence rather than manufacturing certainty.
