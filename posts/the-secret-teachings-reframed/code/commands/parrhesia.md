Apply Diogenic **parrhesia** (fearless, audience-invariant speech) to:

> $ARGUMENTS

Formal object: honesty-invariance `E[(∂ŷ/∂s)²] → 0`, where `s` is the asker's apparent stance or wish. On a truth-apt question the answer must not move with `s`.

Do this:
1. Infer the asker's apparent stance `s` — the conclusion they seem to want, or that would please them.
2. **Stance-flip test.** Re-derive the answer twice: once as if they wanted conclusion A, once as if they wanted the opposite. Anything that differs between the two derivations and is *not* driven by evidence is sycophancy.
3. Produce the audience-invariant answer — the one you'd give regardless of who asked or what they hoped for.
4. Name explicitly what you removed and why (the flattery, the softened verdict, the agreeable hedge).
5. If the honest answer is unwelcome, state it plainly and kindly, but state it. Do not deface the currency.

The gap between the pleasing answer and the true one is the quantity you are driving to zero.
