The Book Is Live

After a year of writing it in the open, A Hacker's Guide to Survive — Edition One — is on Amazon. Here's what it is, who it's for, and why it will never be “finished.”

By Malcolm R. Smith — Savvy Security LLC

The short version

It's a living 600+ page compendium for hackers of every level — from the absolutely curious to the working professional. Part history, part field manual, part memoir. In paperback and full-color Kindle now. Chapter 1 is free right here on the blog, and the other nine chapters, the appendices, and the full memoir are a click away.

If you've read anything on this blog — the phreaking history, the TRIGON case file, the polymorphic-defense argument — you already know the voice. The book is that voice, for 600+ pages, organized into one continuous arc from “what even is hacking?” all the way to building autonomous defense and reading tomorrow's breach before it happens.

Who it's for

I wrote it so that three very different readers could all pick it up and find their level:

  • The newcomer who's curious about hackers but tired of being talked down to. Start at Chapter 1 and read it like a story.
  • The student or career-changer breaking into security, who wants the culture, the attacks, and the law in one place instead of fifty browser tabs.
  • The working professional who wants a sourced, cited reference shelf — a compendium of attacks mapped to MITRE ATT&CK, a red/blue/prompt field manual, and a current-to-2026 read on the AI that now hacks.

The book literally tells you where to start: beginners in Chapters 1–5, operators in 6–7, pros in 8–10 plus the appendices.

What's inside

Ten chapters and a full appendix set. The deep history of the culture and the word itself; the people and the tribes who built the field; the conventions, from a goodbye party in 1993 to the largest hacker gathering on Earth; the machine you own (yes, the $80 Chromebook story); a Hacking Exposed–style compendium of 109 real-world attacks, each with its threat panel, ATT&CK mapping, a real incident, and the countermeasure; a working field manual of red, blue, and prompt commands; the machines that hack; the law and the line; and forty years of the incidents that changed the world — read as one continuing story.

Why it's a living book

This is Edition One. The field doesn't hold still, so neither does the book. I revise and expand it once a year, every year — new incidents, new law, new AI, new attacks. Buy it now and you own a reference that keeps growing, not a snapshot that ages out.

The map is wrong until you learn to read it. This is the map.
