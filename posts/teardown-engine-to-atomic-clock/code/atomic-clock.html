The quantum, accurately

Caesium-133's single outer electron and its nucleus (spin I=7/2) couple into two hyperfine ground states, F=3 and F=4. The gap between them is fixed by nature. The 2019 SI defines the second by declaring that unperturbed splitting to be exactly 9,192,631,770 Hz — so the clock doesn't measure the second, it is the second.

The clock uses the field-insensitive mF=0 → mF=0 transition, and Ramsey's two separated microwave zones to make the resonance razor-sharp. The detector count peaks dead on resonance; the servo dithers and steers the quartz to that peak.

On the time shown: the apparatus is a faithful physics simulation. The Eastern-time display is read from your device's network-synchronised clock and formatted to America/New_York (it handles EST/EDT itself). Stability figures are representative of commercial caesium beam standards (~1×10⁻¹² at 1 s; fountains reach ~10⁻¹⁶).
