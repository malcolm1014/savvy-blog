For the person walking in cold, and for the veteran who stayed. The history, the people, the tribes, the cons, the lab, the attacks, the commands, the defense, the machines that now hack alongside us, the law, and the ten we survived.

How to use this book

This is not a book you read front to back, though you can. It is a compendium — a reference you keep open on the second monitor. It is built for hackers of every level: the first three chapters are orientation anyone can read on a phone; the middle chapters are a working lab manual and an attack-and-defense reference you will actually type from; the last chapters are the history, the law, and the hard-won judgment that keep a thirty-year career out of a courtroom.

Three commitments run through every page. It is sourced — researchers are quoted in their own words, academic papers are cited, and primary documents are linked. It leans on the new tools honestly — cloud-based labs and AI-assisted (“vibe”) workflows are treated as what they are in 2026: the way the work is actually done now. And it is alive — a new edition ships once a year, every year, so the attack compendium, the project lists, and the news never go stale.

The Compendium

  1. 01 The Word and the Fire Published A deep history of hacker culture — from an MIT clubroom in 1959 through phreaking, Homebrew, the ethic, the underground, the Crackdown, the Chaos Computer Club, the Crypto Wars, free software, and the long road to going legit. continues: The Word Was Ours First · GNU at 43
  2. 02 The People Published The key figures, in their own words — the founders (Stallman, Thompson & Ritchie, Torvalds, Woz), the cryptographers (Diffie, Hellman, Zimmermann, Marlinspike), the outlaws who came back (Draper, Mitnick, Poulsen), the defenders (Schneier, Mudge, Moussouris, Kaminsky, Rutkowska), the keepers (Goldstein, Moss, Long), the makers (Huang, Barnaby Jack, Miller & Valasek), the next machine (Hotz), and the lost (Gonzalez, Swartz, Hutchins). continues: Built on Rock and Roll
  3. 03 The Tribes Published The major groups and crews, read as trust and communications-security organizations — the 1980s crews (Legion of Doom, Masters of Deception), the supergroups (cDc, L0pht), the Chaos Computer Club, the defenders’ collectives (the Honeynet Project), the faceless movements (Anonymous, LulzSec), the nation-state APTs (Equation Group, APT1, Fancy Bear, Lazarus), and the ransomware syndicates (Conti, REvil, LockBit). continues: The Trust Problem
  4. 04 The Gatherings Published A deep field guide to the conventions — DEF CON and its villages, Black Hat, ShmooCon, BSides, HackMiami and the regionals, HOPE and the 2600 meetings, the Chaos Communication Congress — and the ancestors they descend from: USENET, the BBS, and the Linux user groups. continues: Hack the Handshake
  5. 05 The Machine You Own Published An entire research company on a used $80 Chromebook: vibe coding (honestly, with the research on where AI helps and hurts), a two-model AI stack, Hetzner cloud labs to build and debug, open-source and open-hardware, and the real path from a cheap laptop to a DARPA proposal — SAM.gov, SBIR/STTR, and a university partner. continues: The DEFCON 34 Baochip · The Long Way In
  6. 06 The Compendium of Attacks Published The thickest chapter: a Hacking Exposed–style compendium of 109 known attacks across twenty domains — recon, scanning, social engineering, web/OWASP, network, exploitation, credentials, privilege escalation, persistence, evasion, C2 & exfiltration, wireless, physical, mobile, cloud/container, OT/ICS/IoT, AI/LLM, crypto, and supply-chain/insider — each with how it acts, a Popularity/Simplicity/Impact/Risk threat panel, its MITRE ATT&CK mapping, a real incident, and the countermeasure, closing on the handful of controls that stop most of it.
  7. 07 The Field Manual Published Red, blue, and prompt in one manual — full, working commands and what each one does, organized for the jobs that run them. The offensive chain (recon, exploitation, post-exploitation, pivoting), the defensive chain (triage, log hunting, network defense, forensics), and a Prompt Field Manual of Google-Fu search operators and prompt-engineering commands, closing on a map from each security job to the sections it lives in. continues: RTFM & BTFM, combined
  8. 08 The Machines That Hack Published AI in offense and defense, deeply sourced and current to 2026 — from the 2016 DARPA Cyber Grand Challenge stage to the 2025 AI Cyber Challenge final; autonomous CTF and pentest agents (XBOW, Cybench, EnIGMA); defensive AI finding real zero-days (Big Sleep); the MIT and academic literature; nation-state AI (Anthropic’s GTG-1002); AI-powered Sybil attacks and data poisoning; and the vibe-coded arsenal. continues: The Ghost in the Shellcode
  9. 09 The Law and the Line Published Cyber law in depth — the CFAA and Van Buren, the DMCA, wiretap, surveillance, export controls and the world’s laws (Budapest, the UN Cybercrime Convention, the UK Computer Misuse Act, Germany’s 202c, NIS2, GDPR, the EU Cyber Resilience Act, China, India); the landmark cases hackers won and lost (Bernstein, Van Buren, hiQ, Sklyarov vs. Morris, Swartz, Gonzalez, DeCSS, the Sullivan cover-up, Coalfire); the Electronic Frontier Foundation; coordinated disclosure and safe harbor; the white hat’s do’s and don’ts; a call for more hackers in law; and the full path to working for Uncle Sam. Not legal advice. continues: The Word Was Ours First
  10. 10 The News and the Reckoning Planned The incidents that changed the world — Morris, Stuxnet, Slammer, Conficker, Heartbleed, Mirai, WannaCry, NotPetya, SolarWinds, Log4Shell and what came after — and how to read the news like a defender. continues: The Ten We Survived
  11. A Appendices Planned A working glossary (the jargon file, annotated), an annual convention calendar, copy-paste lab recipes, and the reading shelf every chapter points back to.

On sources, quotations, and pictures. Every quotation in this book is attributed to the person or document it came from, and every factual claim is traceable to the sources listed at the end of each chapter. Where a line appears in quotation marks it is a direct quote from the cited source or a verified public record of it; where a view is attributed without quotation marks it is a faithful paraphrase of a documented public statement, not a verbatim transcript. All cover art and diagrams are original vector work by Savvy Security LLC; the book uses portraits only from properly licensed, correctly credited sources, and omits them otherwise.

This is Edition One. A new edition ships once a year. Chapters marked Planned are being written and researched now; the status badges above update as each one is published.
